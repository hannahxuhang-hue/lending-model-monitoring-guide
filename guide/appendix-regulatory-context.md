# Appendix: regulatory context (as of September 2026)

> A summary of public sources for orientation. Not legal or regulatory advice. Check the current text and
> talk to counsel or your examiner about what applies to your institution.

## Model risk management guidance

- **Revised interagency guidance, April 17, 2026.** The Federal Reserve (SR 26-2), OCC (Bulletin 2026-13), and
  FDIC issued revised model risk management guidance. It rescinded the 2011 guidance (SR 11-7 / OCC 2011-12)
  and related OCC issuances. It states that it is expected to be most relevant to banking organizations
  with **over $30 billion in total assets**, and may be relevant to smaller institutions with significant model
  risk exposure. It describes ongoing monitoring as evaluating "the extent to which a model is performing as
  expected given potential changes in products, exposures, activities, clients, data relevance, or market
  conditions," and says the frequency and scope of validation activities vary with the model and its
  constraints. Generative and agentic AI are outside its scope. NCUA did not join.
- **OCC Bulletin 2025-26 (October 6, 2025)** clarified that community banks are not required to perform annual
  model validations and should tailor model risk management to their size and complexity.

**What this means for small lenders.** The detailed federal guidance is now explicitly aimed at large banks.
Community banks are told to tailor. Credit unions are outside the interagency guidance. Non-depository CDFI loan
funds are not supervised by the banking agencies at all. For most of the institutions this guide is written for, there
is **no prescriptive monitoring standard**. There is the underlying expectation, repeated in every version of
the guidance, that a lender understands its models and checks that they keep working. This guide is one way to
meet that expectation at a scale a small institution can sustain.

## Fair lending

- **Regulation B amendments (CFPB final rule, published April 22, 2026; effective July 21, 2026).** The rule
  removes the effects test from Regulation B and states that ECOA does not recognize disparate-impact liability.
  It expressly preserves disparate-treatment liability, including where facially neutral criteria are used as
  proxies for a protected characteristic. Commentators note that the Fair Housing Act (for housing-related
  credit), state laws (several of which, including New York's, recognize effects-based claims), prudential
  examination procedures, and private litigation continue to operate independently.
- **BISG.** The CFPB's 2014 methodology paper and code describe the Bayesian Improved Surname Geocoding approach
  used in chapter 5.

## Why small institutions need practical material

The Federal Reserve's 2025 CDFI Survey found that 76% of credit union and bank CDFI respondents identified
technology as the top factor limiting their ability to meet demand, and respondents cited limited ability to
bridge skills gaps through training.
